## (b) K6 REPAIR REVIEW - COLD

### Cold rederivation of the local route

For a hop over horizon `tau`, let `q = pi(x)`, `d = dist(x,G)`, and
`s = d_G(q,y)`. On the first half, use the linear normal path from `x`
to `q`; on the second, use the constant-speed ground geodesic from `q`
to `y`. Because `grad E = 0` on `G` and
`|grad E| <= L dist(.,G)`, the deterministic control
`u = phi' + grad E(phi)` satisfies, up to the declared norm-equivalence
factor,

```text
integral_0^tau |u|^2
  <= (4/tau + L^2 tau/3) d^2 + (2/tau) s^2.
```

Under the controlled measure, the deviation from `phi` obeys a Gronwall
bound with a local vector-field Lipschitz constant
`K = sup ||Hess E||` on the tube. A Brownian tube small enough to land in
`B_{h/2}(y)` has probability bounded below by a positive constant only
when `beta h^2 >= B`. Girsanov then gives the intended
`exp[-beta C(d^2+s^2)]` event cost. This rederives the local mechanism and
also exposes why one `beta_0` independent of all positive `h` cannot be
correct.

For a density, the valid continuation is a localized (or killed) kernel
on a ball/tube with margin: use the half-time event bound, then a genuine
small-noise Brownian-bridge/Girsanov or parametrix lower bound. The local
drift contributes an additional `O(beta h^2)` term because it vanishes on
`G`; it cannot be omitted from a generic bounded-drift estimate. With
`beta >= B/h^2` and explicit local regularity/reach constants, the desired
`beta^(dim M/2) exp[-beta C(delta^2+h^2)]` density form is viable.

For chaining, the Stage-C `B_{h/2}` landing can close the iteration only
with geometric slack. On the declared totally geodesic flat orbits,
projection is locally nonexpansive. Choose consecutive ground centers at
intrinsic distance at most `h/2`; then a point in `B_{h/2}(y_j)` has
`d_G(pi(x),y_{j+1}) <= h`. This uses at most
`ceil(2 D_G/h)` hops, not the displayed `ceil(D_G/h)`. With
`delta = h/2`, the accumulated rate is `O(D_G h) + O(h^2)` and the
prefactor is `c_0^n`; for every fixed `eta > 0`, choosing `h = O(eta)`
does yield a beta-free finite time and a lower bound
`c(eta) exp(-beta eta)` once `beta >= beta_0(eta) = O(eta^-2)`.

Thus the X3 global-cost example is respected: the repair spreads a fixed
tangential distance through many local hops in beta-independent time; it
does not make the one-step global cost vanish.
