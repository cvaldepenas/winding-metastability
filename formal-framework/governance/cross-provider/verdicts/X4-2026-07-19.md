## SOURCE DOSSIER: EXACT PINS AND MINIMUM STATEMENTS

### S1 — weighted heat kernel and pointwise detailed balance

Grigor'yan defines a weighted manifold by `d mu = h^2 d vol` and gives

`Delta_mu = Delta + 2 <grad h / h, grad>`

in §2.1, equation (2.4), author PDF p. 4 (published pagination p. 96). Theorem 3.3 in §3.2, author PDF pp. 11–12 (published pagination pp. 103–104), gives a unique smooth regular heat kernel, symmetry `r_t(x,y)=r_t(y,x)`, and the semigroup identity. “Regular” is defined immediately before the theorem to be positive and sub-Markov. Theorem 3.9, author PDF p. 14 (published p. 106), supplies local strict-positive short-time asymptotics; connectedness plus the semigroup identity propagates positivity. See the [exact-text author PDF](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/~grigor/wma.pdf) and [AMS DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/398/07486).

For the corpus, take `d mu_beta = rho_beta d vol`, `rho_beta = Z_beta^-1 exp(-beta E)`, and `h = sqrt(rho_beta)`. Then

`Delta_mu_beta = Delta - beta grad E . grad`, hence `L_beta = beta^-1 Delta_mu_beta`.

If `r_{t/beta}` is the kernel relative to `mu_beta`, the volume-density kernel is

`p_t(x,y) = r_{t/beta}(x,y) rho_beta(y)`.

Symmetry of `r` gives, pointwise,

`rho_beta(x) p_t(x,y) = rho_beta(y) p_t(y,x)`.

This also verifies the corpus's density-measure conversion; self-adjointness alone would only give an almost-everywhere kernel identity until regularity/continuity is added.

### S2 — finite-window LDP, including `beta`-dependent drift

Freidlin–Wentzell Chapter 5, Theorem 3.2, pp. 135–136, treats a diffusion on a `C^3` manifold whose local generator is

`b_epsilon . grad + (epsilon^2/2) a_ij partial_ij`.

It expressly allows `b_epsilon -> b` uniformly in the uniform coordinate charts and concludes that the action built from the **limit drift** `b` is the sample-path action functional, uniformly in the initial point. Its hypotheses also require bounded/uniformly continuous limiting coefficients and uniform ellipticity. See the official [Springer 3rd-edition record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-25847-3), Chapter 5, pp. 135–136.

The corpus SDE has `sqrt(2/beta)` noise. Put

`epsilon_FW^2 = 2/beta`.

For SR-M1,

`b_beta = b_infinity + beta^-1 d = b_infinity + (epsilon_FW^2/2) d`,

so Theorem 3.2 applies directly. Its source action `S = (1/2) int |dot phi-b_infinity|^2` and exponent `epsilon_FW^-2 S` equal the corpus action/exponent `beta (1/4) int |dot phi-b_infinity|^2`. This is the exact pin for the limit-drift LDP; no separate exponential-equivalence theorem is needed.

### S3 — compact attractors, domains, and exponential iteration

The Chapter 4 exit-time theorems are **not** the right direct citation for the corpus's circles/tori. Chapter 4, Theorems 4.1–4.2, pp. 107–110, assume an asymptotically stable equilibrium point `O`. A circle or torus is not `O`.

The exact general route is Chapter 6:

- Chapter 6 begins at p. 142 by assuming the family satisfies Chapter 5, Theorem 3.2 and uses its limit-drift action.
- Condition (A), p. 150, assumes a smooth-boundary domain with compact closure and finitely many compacta `K_i` such that: points in one `K_i` are zero-quasipotential equivalent; no point of `K_i` is equivalent to a point outside it; and every omega-limit set lying in `D union boundary(D)` is contained in a `K_i`.
- Lemmas 1.7–1.9, pp. 148–150, provide the subexponential neighborhood-excursion and time-control estimates used in long-time iteration.
- Lemma 2.1, pp. 152–153, gives compactum-uniform one-step transition bounds. In particular, the probability of a neighborhood excursion from `K_i` exiting through `boundary(D)` is bracketed at exponential cost `V_tilde_D(K_i,boundary(D))`, uniformly over the prescribed starting neighborhood.
- Lemma 2.2, p. 156, supplies the corresponding first-step estimates for compact starting sets away from the `K_i` and the boundary.
- Theorem 5.3, pp. 176–177, gives `epsilon_FW^2 log E_x tau_D = W_D-M_D(x)`, uniformly for `x` in every compact subset of `D`.

For one attracting compactum `G`, the graph formula reduces explicitly to

`W_D = V_D(G,boundary(D))`, `M_D(x)=0`

for starts attracted to `G`; thus Theorem 5.3 gives the standard single-well mean exponent without making `G` a point. The early-exit probability form follows from the uniform one-excursion upper bound in Chapter 6, Lemma 2.1 (and Lemma 2.2 for initial relocation) by the strong-Markov renewal calculation printed in the proof of Chapter 4, Theorem 4.2, pp. 109–110. This is a source-result composition, not a claim that Chapter 4 itself covers a Morse–Bott set.

No Morse nondegeneracy is present in Condition (A). For each corpus circle/torus `G`, its stated rows map to Condition (A) as follows:

1. `G` is compact, connected, and the limit drift vanishes on `G`. Traversing a fixed path in `G` over time `T` costs `O(1/T)`, so every two points of `G` are equivalent in both directions.
2. `E=e_0` exactly on `G`; the corpus's whole-path action inequality gives `V_D(G,y) >= E(y)-e_0 > 0` for `y notin G`. Hence no point outside `G` is equivalent to it.
3. The corpus's closure-attraction row places every omega-limit set in `G` and excludes boundary rest points.
4. A compact ambient torus gives compact closure. A regular level boundary (`grad E != 0`, or equivalently no boundary rest point for a gradient drift) gives the smooth boundary required on p. 150.

For gradient energy `E`, the source quasipotential is `2(cap-e_0)` in the source normalization. Because `epsilon_FW^-2=beta/2`, the physical exponent is exactly `beta(cap-e_0)`. For any corpus start `q`, `cap-e_0 >= cap-E(q)`; for any compact start set `F`, `cap-e_0 >= cap-max_F E`. Thus the cited compact-attractor theorem is stronger than the one-sided exponents actually consumed.
